The Car

The car I'll be using for my track days is a 2009 Porsche Cayman (987.2). Right now, the car is completely stock and I have big plans for it!

Why the 987.2?

The 987.2 Cayman (2009-2012) sits in a sweet spot in Porsche history. It is the revised 987 platform, so the engine has no issues with bore-scoring or IMS bearings while also coming in a slighly larger size, a 2.9 L flat six! This engine is also the last port injection engine Porsche made, even the 987.2 Cayman S moved to direct injection. While direct injection gives better performance, the port injection gives me peace of mind that my valve are full of carbon build-up. Lastly, the 987.2 has hydraulic steering, allowing me to get a ton of road feel through the steering.

Planned Modifications

Brakes

One of the biggest icks of the Cayman is the brake pedal is very long. At first, I thought this was due to bulging brake lines or needing the lines bled, but apparently this is just how it is on the Cayman. Due to this, a very popular modification is to install the brake master cylinder from the Porsche 997, the 911 of this generation. Along with this, I plan on getting braided steel brake lines and new rotors & pads.

Suspension

With 73,000 miles on the car, the suspension is feeling a little tired. It's not in dire need, but the front strut bearings are creaking and while replacing those, I might as well refresh the rest of the suspension. I thought a lot about what route to take and after a lot of reading, I decided on the Cayman R springs with simple Koni shocks. While I do plan on bringing the car to the track, I really don't think I will benefit much from the infinite adjustablity of coilovers, so it would better to save the money for more track time or other mods.
