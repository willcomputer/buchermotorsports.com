The Car

The car I'll be using for my track days is my 2013 Mazda MX-5 Miata. I've owned this car since Spring 2020, and I love driving it so much. I bought it bone-stock with 18,900 miles on the odometer. After over six years of ownership, it's now sitting at 52,000 miles and running great.

Why the Miata?

I was going to do a write-up on why I love the Miata and why it's the perfect car to learn to drive fast, but I don't need to beat a dead horse. There are a million articles online for this exact topic.

Current Modifications

In my time owning the Miata, I've made a couple of modifications to it for just my street driving pleasure. I did always have track aspirations for this car, so every existing modification will help me on track.

Rims + Tires

When I bought the car, the stock rims had a slight bend in them, so I was forced to get new ones pretty quickly. The obvious choice was the Enkei RPF-1s. I swear to God, these rims were designed with the NC Miata in mind. They look fantastic! Wrapped around those beauties are Continental ExtremeContact DWS06 tires, the best rain tires I've ever used.

Exhaust

Another modification I made was upgrading to the Goodwin Racing RoadsterSport II muffler. Since this car was my daily driver for three years, I didn't want to go crazy with the sound, and this muffler hits the perfect sweet spot. When I get on the throttle, you get a ton of noise in the cabin, but there's zero drone while cruising. I'm not sure how loud it is for bystanders. I've never been there to experience it :D

Suspension

The third and final modification I've made to my Miata is a set of Öhlins Road & Track coilovers. Even though the car only had 50,000 miles, the suspension felt more tired than it should have. Knowing this car had track time in its future, I decided to ball out and get the Öhlins coilovers. I'm so happy I did because it handles like a dream now! It is a little rough on bumpy roads, but I'm not rattling my teeth out of my skull like I might be with a cheaper set.

Future Modifications

With my current modifications, the car is plenty capable for my current driving skill level, so I don't need to do much more to it. The main focus will be improving safety and ensuring I'm in full control of the car.

Roll Bar

Even though my Miata is a power-retractable hardtop (PRHT), the top isn't considered rollover-safe, so I need to add an aftermarket roll bar. The PRHT makes this tricky since it has less clearance than its soft-top counterpart. Luckily for me, a company in Poland called Cybul makes a roll bar specifically for the PRHT NC Miata that maximizes roll bar height while still allowing the top to function. This is great because I can still drive the car in any conditions. It'll definitely come in handy driving around North Carolina with its blazing-hot summer sun.

Seats + Harnesses

Stock seats and seatbelts are perfectly fine for lower-level HPDE sessions for most people. However, they won't cut it for me. Being a paraplegic, I don't have control over my abdominal or trunk muscles. This makes it impossible for me to brace my body into corners, which is something you have to do with stock seats. Even most able-bodied people say that getting racing seats is a huge upgrade because it's something they no longer have to worry about and can instead focus 100% on driving.

So, with all that in mind, I'll be getting at least one racing seat with a 6-point harness for my driver's seat. I'll eventually do the passenger seat, too, but probably not right away. I'm not made of money!

And that's all, folks!

Like I said, in terms of car modifications, not much is planned right now. The majority of my racing funds are going toward maintenance, like brake pads and tires, as well as paying for track time.
