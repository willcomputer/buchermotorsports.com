The Car

The car I'll be using for my track days is a 2009 Porsche Cayman (987.2). Right now, the car is completely stock and I have big plans for it!

Why the 987.2?

The 987.2 Cayman (2009–2012) sits in a sweet spot in Porsche history. It is the revised 987 platform, so it comes with great reliability changes like no more bore scoring on the cylinder wall or IMS bearings issues. The engine is also slightly upgraded, moving from a 2.7 L to a slightly larger 2.9 L flat-six! This engine is also the last port-injection engine Porsche made; even the 987.2 Cayman S moved to direct injection. While direct injection might give better performance, the port injection gives me peace of mind that my valves are not full of carbon buildup. Lastly, the 987.2 has hydraulic steering, allowing me to get a ton of road feel through the steering. This is another hydraulic system to maintain, I think it is worth the added maintenance.

Planned Modifications

Brakes

One of the biggest icks of the Cayman is that the brake pedal travel is very long. At first, I thought this was due to bulging brake lines or needing the lines bled, but apparently, this is just how it is on the Cayman. Due to this, a very popular modification is to install the brake master cylinder from the Porsche 997, the 911 of this generation. Along with this, I plan on getting braided steel brake lines and new rotors & pads.

Suspension

With 73,000 miles on the car, the suspension is feeling a little tired. It's not in dire need, but the front strut bearings are creaking, and while replacing those, I might as well refresh the rest of the suspension. I thought a lot about what route to take, and after a lot of reading, I decided on the Cayman R springs with simple Koni shocks. While I do plan on bringing the car to the track, I really don't think I will benefit much from the infinite adjustability of coilovers, so it would be better to save the money for more track time or other mods.
